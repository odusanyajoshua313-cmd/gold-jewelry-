# Goldplugdami landing page strategy

**Research date:** 3 October 2026  
**Status:** Strategy only. No page or design has been built.

## Executive direction

Plan the site around one measurable action: a qualified WhatsApp conversation that leads to a booking or purchase. A visitor should understand within seconds what Goldplugdami sells or does, who it serves, where it serves them, why it is trustworthy, and what to send on WhatsApp to take the next step.

Goldplugdami is a gold jewelry and gold bar retailer based at Tejuosho, Yaba, Lagos, serving customers across Nigeria through nationwide delivery. The owner publishes separate daily selling rates per gram for 18 Karat, 21 Karat, 22 Karat, and 24 Karat. Each item's final price is calculated as its applicable Karat selling rate per gram multiplied by its weight in grams. Phase 1 is a WhatsApp-assisted storefront with an admin-only workspace for rates, products, and stock; checkout is a future phase. The brand will supply its logo and product images.

### Pattern worth adopting

1. **Hero:** one clear offer, one primary action (WhatsApp), and one optional secondary action (view collection, call, or directions).
2. **Proof immediately after the promise:** authentic customer feedback, real product/service photos, certifications or sourcing documentation where relevant, and verifiable years/volume claims.
3. **Offer clarity:** what is available, how it is priced or quoted, what is custom, and what happens after the customer contacts the business.
4. **Trust information:** materials/purity or specifications, certificates, care/warranty, delivery/pickup, payment options, and a real local identity.
5. **Repeated contact opportunity:** WhatsApp near the hero, after key product/benefit sections, and in a mobile sticky action, without obstructing content.

Baymard’s jewelry and watches research highlights the need to explain fit and feel, product imagery, variations, reviews, and returns through the shopping journey. Its public summary is [here](https://baymard.com/research/jewelry-and-watches). This supports treating photography, dimensions/material facts, and policy access as conversion content rather than decoration.

## 2. Visitor and conversion strategy

### Likely visitor questions

- Is this the kind of product/service I want, and is it available now?
- Is the price clear, or how is a quote calculated?
- Is the gold genuine, what is the purity/weight, and can the claim be verified?
- Can I see real photos, video, or examples on people?
- Is this business real and reachable? Where is it based, and do they serve my area?
- How do delivery, pickup, payment, returns, repairs, custom orders, or appointments work?
- What exactly will happen after I tap WhatsApp, and how soon will someone reply?

The page should answer as many as possible before asking the visitor to message. For expensive or trust-sensitive offers, proof and process clarity are key parts of the sales message.

### WhatsApp journey

Use a direct `wa.me` click-to-chat URL with the international-format business number, no `+`, spaces, or punctuation, and a short prefilled message. Example template (fill only once the offer is confirmed):

> Hi Goldplugdami, I found you on your website. I’m interested in [product/service]. Could you share availability, price, and next steps?

For bookings, use a booking-oriented message with slots or service selection, rather than implying that the website calendar has reserved an appointment when it has not. Set expectations (“We usually reply during [hours]”) only if the team can meet them. The official [WhatsApp guidance for adding WhatsApp to a Google Business Profile](https://faq.whatsapp.com/712818078160617) says an eligible profile can display a WhatsApp click-to-chat option in Search and Maps; it requires a valid WhatsApp Business number in international format and a click-to-chat URL.

Suggested conversation handling:

1. Prefilled opener carries source, product/service interest, and booking intent.
2. WhatsApp Business greeting explains reply hours and asks one easy qualifying question.
3. Quick replies cover price/quote, stock, custom work, delivery, location, and booking.
4. Use labels for new inquiry, qualified, booked, paid, completed, and follow-up.
5. Track which CTA was clicked and which source brought the lead; do not upload private chat content to analytics.

Measure: WhatsApp CTA clicks, conversations started, qualified inquiries, bookings/orders, response time, and completed sales. A click is not a conversion by itself.

## 3. Proposed design-system direction (not page design)

The system should signal **premium, credible, personal, and locally grounded**. “Gold” alone is a predictable category cue; the brand needs its own recognizable signature (for example, a distinctive dark ink tone, warm neutral, editorial serif, and restrained metallic accent). Do not choose final values until the logo, audience, photography, and product category are confirmed.

### Tokens to define in the next phase

- **Color roles:** primary brand, dark/ink, warm background, body text, muted text, border, WhatsApp action green, success/error. Gold should be an accent, not small gold text on white; maintain accessible contrast.
- **Type roles:** expressive display face for short headlines; highly legible sans serif for body/UI; numeric style for prices/specifications. Define desktop/mobile scale and line lengths.
- **Spacing/layout:** 4 or 8 px base rhythm; narrow readable text column; generous image-led sections; responsive gutters and breakpoints.
- **Shape and elevation:** a limited radius set, subtle borders/shadows, consistent button heights and focus rings.
- **Imagery rules:** real products, close detail, scale/on-body context, authentic people/process/location, consistent lighting and color treatment; optimize crops for mobile. Avoid generic stock gold imagery as primary proof.
- **Components:** header, primary/secondary button, WhatsApp button, product/service card, proof/review card, specification strip, FAQ accordion, location/contact block, footer, sticky mobile CTA.
- **Accessibility:** semantic headings, keyboard access, visible focus, descriptive alt text, readable contrast, reduced motion support, and usable tap targets.

### Voice and message system

Create one message hierarchy: **what Goldplugdami offers → specific customer benefit → evidence → next step**. Avoid unsupported superlatives (“best,” “100% authentic,” “trusted by thousands”) unless proof exists. Translate trade language (karat, purity, weight, service steps) into customer language, with specifics retained.

## 4. Recommended landing-page content plan

This is a future content blueprint only; no design or copy is being produced yet.

1. **Header:** logo/name, 2–4 useful anchors, compact WhatsApp CTA.
2. **Hero:** offer + audience/location + real visual + WhatsApp action + a small trust cue.
3. **Fast proof row:** 3–4 true differentiators (e.g., material verification, custom work, local pickup, delivery coverage).
4. **What’s available:** strongest categories/services or booking reasons; each has an obvious next step.
5. **Proof in context:** customer reviews with permission, customer/product photos, certifications or process evidence.
6. **How it works:** 3 steps from inquiry to selection/booking to payment/delivery/visit.
7. **About the maker/business:** short human story and real team/workshop/store image.
8. **Practical details:** price or quote method, location/service area, hours, delivery, payment, warranty/returns, care, booking policy.
9. **FAQ:** answer top objections and long-tail search questions.
10. **Final WhatsApp CTA + footer:** direct contact, socials, address/service area, hours, policies, legal/business identity.

If the primary offer is a consultation or appointment rather than direct products, replace the catalog section with consultation types, what the customer should bring, duration, location/virtual option, fees/deposit, and rescheduling terms.

## 5. Google Search, Maps, and local discovery

### Maps and “registering the landing page”

Websites are not separately registered on Google Maps. The relevant route is an eligible **Google Business Profile** representing the business; the website is added to that profile. Google’s [eligibility and representation rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say a business needs a customer-visit location or to travel to customers. A purely online business without in-person contact may not qualify. Use the real-world name consistently, provide an accurate address or service area, and never invent a storefront or virtual office.

For a qualifying business:

- Create/claim and verify the profile; use the most accurate primary category and only relevant secondary categories.
- Keep name, phone, website URL, hours, address/service area, and business description consistent with the landing page and social profiles.
- Add useful original photos, products/services, booking/contact links where available, and respond professionally to reviews.
- If a public address should remain private for a service-area business, follow Google’s address-hiding and service-area rules.
- Add WhatsApp as a chat option when the profile feature is available for the business/account.

Google says local visibility mainly reflects **relevance, distance, and prominence**; complete information, reviews, photos, and accurate categories can help, but there is no way to request or pay for a higher local ranking. See [Google’s local ranking guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091/improve-your-local-ranking-on-google?hl=en-GB).

### Organic SEO and brand search

1. **Own the brand query:** exact consistent brand name, good page title/H1, About/contact details, social profiles, and a connected verified Search Console property. If alternate spellings are commonly used, mention them naturally in a short FAQ/about context, not as keyword stuffing.
2. **Search intent:** research actual phrases customers use once location and offer are known (e.g. product + city, custom gold jewelry + city, booking/price/delivery questions). Build useful dedicated pages only for distinct offers/locations; do not create thin near-duplicate city pages.
3. **On-page basics:** unique title and description, one clear H1, descriptive headings, crawlable text, useful image alt text, descriptive internal links, clean URLs, canonical URL, and consistent NAP details (name/address/phone).
4. **Technical discovery:** HTTPS, mobile-first rendering, indexable HTML, robots and canonical checks, XML sitemap submitted in Search Console, Google Business Profile website link, and URL Inspection after launch. Google’s [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains titles, links, and Search Console; its [developer guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started-developers) covers crawlability and sitemap submission.
5. **Structured data:** use accurate `Organization` and, if eligible/appropriate, the most specific `LocalBusiness` subtype with verified address/contact/hours. Structured data helps Google understand the entity; it does not guarantee a special result or higher rank. Follow Google’s [LocalBusiness structured data documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business).
6. **Authority and reputation:** earn real local mentions/links from partners, associations, press, events, and relevant community sites; ask actual customers for honest reviews without incentives or gating; respond to reviews. Never buy fake reviews or links.
7. **Content:** publish helpful answers around the actual customer journey (purity/quality checks, custom ordering, care, delivery, price/quote factors) only where those topics fit the business and can be answered accurately.

No one can promise a top Google position. Branded searches are usually a more controllable early goal than competitive generic terms. Discovery and ranking take time and depend on location, relevance, competition, authority, and business eligibility.

## 6. Technical plan and tool choices

Choose the stack after confirming whether Goldplugdami needs a catalog, inventory/pricing, payments, booking, and who will maintain content.

| Need | Practical choice | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| One campaign page, WhatsApp leads, frequently changing copy | Webflow, Framer, or a simple static site | Fast launch with low maintenance and no complex product inventory. |
| Product catalog, stock, variants, cart/payments | Shopify | Better when products and checkout are central; WhatsApp remains a contact/assisted-sales path. |
| Bespoke design or complex content/integrations | Next.js or Astro + managed hosting/CMS | Use when there is a real need for custom behavior and a maintainer is available. |
| Appointment scheduling | Link to a real booking service or a simple request flow | Use when time slots, deposits, reminders, or calendar sync are important. WhatsApp chat alone does not reserve a slot. |

Suggested minimum instrumentation: Google Search Console, privacy-conscious analytics or GA4 if appropriate, campaign UTM tags on social/ads, tracked WhatsApp click events, and a simple lead outcome log. Provide a privacy notice and consent controls where required by the target market and tracking setup.

Performance targets: at the 75th percentile aim for LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, and CLS ≤ 0.1. These are the “good” Core Web Vitals thresholds in [web.dev’s current guidance](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds?hl=en). Use responsive compressed images, specify image dimensions, avoid heavy autoplay media and unnecessary third-party scripts, and test on mid-range mobile plus slow connections.

Security and operations: HTTPS, domain ownership under the business, MFA on registrar/hosting/Google/WhatsApp accounts, backups, accessible contact ownership, form spam protection if forms exist, and a documented update process.

## 8. Success measures

- **Primary:** qualified WhatsApp inquiries that turn into bookings/orders.
- **Funnel:** landing sessions → WhatsApp clicks → chats started → qualified leads → confirmed booking/order → fulfilled sale.
- **Quality:** response time, booking completion rate, average order value or booking value, lead source, and common drop-off reason.
- **Search/local:** branded impressions/clicks, non-brand local impressions, Search Console indexing, profile views/actions, direction requests/calls where relevant, review volume and quality.
- **Experience:** mobile CTA success, page speed/Core Web Vitals, accessibility defects, and customer understanding from real conversations.

Set numeric targets only after a baseline or reliable business economics are known.

### Updated positioning and customer decision path

The confirmed assortment serves two related but distinct shopping intents:

1. **Wearable gold jewelry:** choose a type (ring, bangle, earrings, neck chain), design, karat, size/length, weight, price, and availability.
2. **Gold bars:** choose bar weight and purity, then evaluate authenticity documentation, price basis, verification, and delivery/security.

The first screen should say Goldplugdami sells gold jewelry and bars, the available Karat options, and Lagos/Nigeria delivery. Then let visitors choose **Shop Jewelry** or **Shop Gold Bars**. Avoid positioning the brand as custom jewelry unless bespoke production is also a real service.

The main questions to answer on-page are: Is it real gold? What karat and weight is it? What is the price and how long is that quote valid? Is the item in stock? What happens after I pay? How is it delivered safely? Can I verify it on arrival? Use accurate business evidence and written policy rather than broad trust slogans.

### Updated visual and interaction priorities

Retain the calm premium visual and motion rules from section 16, but make the interface behave like a useful gold catalog rather than a custom studio portfolio:

- **Visual hierarchy:** primary emphasis on real product photography, karat/weight/price facts, category browsing, and availability. Use gold as a finish accent; use dark ink for legible specifications and a high-contrast WhatsApp action.
- **Product filters:** category, karat, price band, and availability only if inventory is accurate and there are enough products for filters to help. On mobile, filters open in a bottom sheet with selected chips, clear/apply controls, and preserved scroll position.
- **Product image interactions:** tap thumbnails to change the main image; pinch/zoom or open a controlled full-screen gallery; show on-body scale/context alongside product detail. Never put essential purity/weight or price only in an image.
- **Product selection:** variant selector for karat, size, or chain length only when the selected variant’s weight and price update correctly. Clearly mark “price on request” where pricing depends on changing rates.
- **Price states:** show NGN currency formatting consistently. If tied to a changing gold rate, display “price updated [time/date, WAT]” and quote validity only if operationally true. If price is manually maintained, use a “last updated” note or quote CTA rather than claiming a live rate.
- **WhatsApp CTA:** product-level “Ask about this item,” catalog-level “Chat to order,” and persistent mobile WhatsApp action; keep the product identifier in prefilled text. Use WhatsApp green only for the action, not every interface accent.
- **Cart vs WhatsApp:** choose one primary purchase route. If the store can actually process stock, price, payments, and fulfillment online, the primary action can be “Add to cart” with WhatsApp as support. If orders are manually confirmed, WhatsApp stays primary and no fake cart/checkout affordance should appear.
- **Scroll and motion:** follow section 16 timing/reduced-motion rules; product browsing should not trigger distracting parallax. Use small fades, clear selected states, filter-count feedback, and brief loading/success messages. Prioritize stable product cards and fast image loading over animation.

The earlier provisional palette can remain a starting hypothesis, but should be adjusted for the actual logo and product photography. Keep the measured contrast relationships rather than relying on pale metallic gold for text. The WCAG 2.2 contrast targets remain 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text, per [W3C guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

### Remaining discovery questions before design

The core offer and geography are now clear. The highest-value remaining facts are:

1. **Physical retail:** Is there a customer-visit shop/showroom in Lagos, and what area/address should be public? Are customers served directly or only through third-party shipping?
2. **Buying flow:** Can visitors pay/order online, or must every order be confirmed on WhatsApp? Does the brand need a real cart, or is an assisted catalog enough?

### Proposed first-screen and page layout

**Desktop, first viewport:**

- Compact top bar: wordmark left; “Work / How it works / Reviews / Visit” anchors; phone or WhatsApp action right.
- Hero split roughly 45/55: left side has a small location/availability eyebrow, a short two-line promise, one sentence that explains the specific offer, a large WhatsApp/booking button, a quieter secondary “See the process” link, and one evidence strip (real rating, appointment hours, or in-house proof). Right side uses one unmistakably real hero image or short silent loop of the actual product/person/process, cropped for a strong focal point.
- Keep headline, primary action, and trust cue visible at common laptop heights. Do not place important copy inside the image.

**Mobile, first viewport:**

- Brand/name and menu at the top; no oversized navigation.
- Headline, short benefit, and a full-width primary button appear before long copy. Show image immediately after or beside this content depending on actual photo quality.
- A fixed bottom action bar can carry “WhatsApp to book” after the hero CTA scrolls out of view. It should have safe-area padding, not obscure form controls, and disappear when the booking form/confirmation is in view.
- Design mobile first because local discovery and WhatsApp opens commonly happen on phones; make the initial CTA thumb-reachable and large.

**Scroll order:** hero → proof strip → offer/category cards → “how it works” process → real-work gallery → reviews → owner/studio/local block → price/timing and policies → FAQ → final booking CTA/footer. Keep each section visually distinct but avoid a different animation gimmick per section.

### Color direction and contrast rules

Provisional luxury palette for exploration (not final brand approval): warm ivory `#F7F1E7`, espresso ink `#211A16`, deep evergreen/WhatsApp `#075E54`, antique gold `#795B25`, champagne metal accent `#C9A45C`, secondary text `#655C53`. On these exact flat colors, approximate WCAG contrast ratios are: espresso on ivory **15.27:1**, deep evergreen button with white label **7.67:1**, antique gold on ivory **5.60:1**, secondary text on ivory **5.82:1**. These are calculated ratios for the hex pairs shown; the final page still needs contrast review for every real state and overlay.

- Use ivory/white for most reading surfaces, ink for headings/body, and gold mainly for thin rules, icons, small labels, or decorative details. Never rely on pale gold text to communicate essential content.
- Use the deep-green WhatsApp fill with white text for the primary action so brand gold does not compete with the recognizable chat action.
- Over photography, put text on a solid/opaque surface or tested gradient; do not assume a shadow makes text readable.
- Target WCAG 2.2 AA: at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. W3C’s [contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) explains those thresholds. Distinguish errors/success with text and icons, not color alone.

### Motion and animation plan

Motion should support attention and orientation, with reduced motion available. Keep the visual feel refined and tactile: reveal a crafted detail, indicate a transition, or confirm an action. Avoid perpetual sparkle, parallax that moves the whole page, long cinematic intros, cursor-following effects, or motion that delays the CTA.

| Moment | Planned behavior | Timing / boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| Initial load | Hero text is present immediately; image can fade in gently. No blocking logo intro or delayed CTA. | 180–280 ms fade/translate by 8–12 px; CTA visible at once. |
| Scroll reveal | Section title and cards fade/raise in once as they enter viewport; stagger cards slightly. | 250–400 ms, 40–80 ms stagger; stop after first reveal and avoid large travel distances. |
| Product/gallery image | On pointer hover only, subtle scale or alternate-detail reveal; on touch, tap opens a simple gallery/lightbox with close and next/previous controls. | 1.02–1.04 scale over 180–240 ms; no hover-only information. |
| Buttons | Color darkens slightly, shadow/border tightens; keyboard focus gets a clear outline; press gives a small tactile shift. | 120–180 ms; pressed translate no more than 1 px. |
| FAQ accordion | Height and icon rotate smoothly; focus remains on the trigger; open/closed state is announced semantically. | 180–240 ms; single or multiple-open behavior chosen based on FAQ length. |
| Form validation | Inline message appears beside the field; invalid field receives icon + text + border; focus moves to first error on submit. | No shake animation; preserve entered values. |
| WhatsApp handoff | Opens WhatsApp in a new app/tab with prefilled message; the site CTA briefly confirms “Opening WhatsApp” and can offer a copy-number fallback. | Immediate; never wait for animation before opening the link. |
| Reduced motion | Remove parallax, large transforms, auto-playing loops, and nonessential reveals; preserve all content and state changes. | Respect the user’s operating-system preference. |

### Interaction and control behavior

- **Primary CTA:** label names the action, e.g. “Book a WhatsApp consultation” or “Ask about this piece.” Use the official WhatsApp icon paired with readable text; do not use an icon-only floating bubble as the sole booking path.
- **Secondary CTA:** “See how custom orders work,” “View the collection,” or “Get directions” based on the true customer task. Avoid equal visual weight between too many buttons.
- **Sticky CTA:** mobile-only or mobile-prioritized, with clear label and accessible close/hide behavior if it blocks content. It should not cover cookie controls, footers, or form submit buttons.
- **Inquiry form:** if qualification is useful, start with name + WhatsApp number + what they want; make budget, style, date, and inspiration optional. Use visible labels, short helper text, a clear privacy note, inline errors, and success state with next-step expectations. If WhatsApp is the main channel, keep the form secondary instead of asking users to submit the same information twice.
- **Image upload:** optional, mobile camera-friendly, accepts common image formats, states maximum file size and privacy/use. Provide WhatsApp alternative.
- **Booking inputs:** only ask for date/time after showing availability or state clearly that this is a request, not a confirmed reservation. Show timezone, location/virtual format, duration, cost/deposit, and rescheduling terms before final submit.
- **Input states:** default, hover, focus-visible, filled, invalid, disabled, and success. Maintain label/helper placement so validation does not cause layout jumps.
- **Reviews:** carousel only if there are enough real reviews; allow manual next/previous and visible pause. A static 2–3 review grid is preferred for short landing pages because it is easier to scan and does not hide proof.
- **Menus and accordions:** keyboard-operable, touch-friendly, and retain focus logically. Avoid scroll-jacking; ordinary browser scroll and anchor links should work.
- **Feedback:** links visibly change on hover/focus, buttons show an in-progress state when needed, forms confirm completion, and outbound WhatsApp links have a sensible fallback if WhatsApp is unavailable.

The page should remain calm and functional if JavaScript animations fail. For touch controls, target at least 44 CSS px where practical; WCAG 2.2 AA’s minimum target-size criterion is 24 CSS px with exceptions, so larger targets are the safer design choice for a primary booking action.
